Sample deal packet

What a deal packet looks like.

SAMPLE — this property and every number on this page are illustrative. This is not a live listing and nothing here is for sale. It shows the exact format a real parcel travels in when it goes out to qualified builders: the property, the recorded-comp logic, the pricing math, and the proposed structure, on one page.

The property (sample)

7.4 acres, vacant industrial land with a fenced yard — Hialeah area, Miami-Dade County, Florida. Zoning IU-2 (industrial). Flat, rectangular, paved yard area, utilities at the street, direct arterial access. Held by the same family entity for 20+ years. Current use: leased informally as truck parking, month to month.

The comps behind the value (sample)

Recorded sales of similar industrial land in the same submarket, most recent first:

· 6.9 acres, 1.2 miles — sold at $398,000 per acre (recorded, 2026)
· 8.1 acres, 2.0 miles — sold at $421,000 per acre (recorded, 2025)
· 5.5 acres, 0.8 miles — sold at $405,000 per acre (recorded, 2025)

Median ≈ $405,000 per acre. That median is what we call builder value: what builders credibly pay for dirt like this, from the record — not an appraisal and not a seller's asking price.

The pricing math (sample)

Builder value: 7.4 acres × $405,000 = $2,997,000
Contract price at 65% of builder value: $1,948,000
Assignment fee, 5% of contract price: $97,400 — paid inside the transaction at closing; the seller's contract price does not change.

The proposed structure (sample)

· Seller signs one purchase contract with Lot & Loaded at the contract price.
· The contract is assigned to a qualified builder from our bench; the assignment is named in the paperwork.
· Earnest money posted on signing; closing cash, as-is, inside 30 days of a clear title commitment.
· Due diligence: zoning and use confirmed against the county record before the packet goes out; environmental and survey are the closing builder's, on the contract clock.

What was verified before this packet went out (sample)

· Ownership: the signing entity matches the county record; the person who signed can sign.
· Zoning and use: industrial use confirmed against the county's published zoning — not the seller's word for it.
· Acreage and access: parcel dimensions and road frontage checked against the property appraiser's record.
· Tenure: held 20+ years by the same entity — long-held, off-market, never listed.
· Liens and taxes: no open tax delinquency found on the county roll at packet date; the title commitment at contract is the final word.

The timeline a real packet runs on (sample)

Day 0 — the packet goes to qualified builders whose buy box it fits, fastest closers first. Days 1–2 — first-look window: a builder can take it, pass it, or ask for the underlying file; a pass is an answer, not a delay. On a take — the seller's contract is signed, earnest money posts, and the assignment to the closing builder is executed with it. Inside 30 days of a clear title commitment — the closing, cash, as-is. If no builder takes the packet inside the window, it comes back to us and we say so plainly: re-priced, re-matched, or released.

Questions builders ask about packets

Is the 65% negotiable? The packet price is the price the seller has agreed to. Our gate priced it off recorded comps; a builder who wants the dirt at a different number is passing, and we move to the next fit.
Who holds the contract before closing? Lot & Loaded signs with the seller and assigns to the closing builder — the assignment is part of the paperwork every party signs, and our fee is paid inside the transaction at closing.
What if title comes back dirty? The contract's own terms govern — cure period, extension, or exit — the same as any commercial purchase. Nobody is asked to close over a title problem in silence.

What happens next on a real deal

The packet goes only to qualified builders whose buy box the parcel fits — fastest closers first, on a stated first-look clock. If a builder passes, we say so and move to the next fit. Get qualified to see packets like this one, or register a parcel if you find the dirt.
