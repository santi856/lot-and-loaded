What we look at

Recorded sales of similar property near yours, the zoning and what can actually be built, acreage and road access, utilities, and what the builders we work with are buying right now. We do not price off tax assessments or online estimates. If your property has environmental questions or title issues, tell us in the notes — surprises found later slow everything down; facts up front usually don't. Inherited property, property behind on taxes, and property held by partners who no longer agree on it are all situations we see; tell us the situation and we will work with it. If we agree a number, the contract is plain: cash, as-is, our assignment to the closing builder named in the paperwork, and a closing date you agree to. No open houses and no strangers walking your property.

Questions, answered straight

What kinds of property do you buy?

Commercial and industrial property: vacant and development land, warehouses and industrial buildings, teardown and redevelopment sites, and outdoor storage yards.

What will you pay for my property?

We price off recorded sales of similar property and what builders actually pay for property like yours — not a guess. If your property fits the builders we work with, you hear back with a real number fast.

Do I pay any fees?

No. Sellers never pay us anything — no listing fees, no showings, no broker fees, nothing out of your pocket.

What happens after I send the form?

We run your property through the same gate as every deal: zoning, acreage, and recorded sales of similar property. If it fits, we come back with a real number. If it doesn't fit, we tell you that instead of going quiet.

Who actually buys my property?

You sign one contract with Lot & Loaded at the agreed price. We assign that contract to a vetted builder from our bench, and the builder closes it — cash, as-is, on the contract date. The assignment is in the paperwork you sign, our fee is paid inside the deal at closing, and your agreed price does not change.

Does sending the form obligate me to sell?

No. Sending the form is not a contract and doesn't obligate you to anything — it just starts the conversation.
